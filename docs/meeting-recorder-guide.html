User Guide & Feature List

Record or upload a meeting and get a written transcript with speakers separated, AI summaries, action items, and notes — all searchable and exportable. Everything runs on the church's own servers; no meeting audio leaves the building.

What it does, in one line each

📝 Transcribes automatically

Upload a phone memo or Zoom recording, or record live in the browser. Speech becomes text with timestamps.

👥 Separates speakers

Each voice gets its own label, then you give those labels real names (Ken, Dana…) and every export uses them.

✨ Summarizes with AI

Key Points, Decisions Made, Action Items (who/what/when), and Open Questions — regenerated on demand.

🗒️ Keeps your notes

Add your own notes beside the transcript; they stay with the meeting and are listed with times.

⬇️ Exports anywhere

Copy, download .txt or .md, or save straight to Google Drive. Every export is logged.

🔒 Private by default

Each person sees only their own meetings. Audio is never downloadable without signing in as its owner.

Quick start (60 seconds)

  1. Open https://meeting.acts19.us and sign in.
  2. Click + New meeting.
  3. Give it a title, then either pick an audio file or click ● Start recording.
  4. Click Upload & transcribe and wait for the status to turn Transcribed ✅.
  5. Open the meeting: name the speakers, read the transcript, click ✨ Summarize with AI, and export.
Works on phone too. The interface is mobile-friendly, so you can record straight from your phone's browser while the meeting is happening — no app to install.

1. Getting started

Signing in

Go to https://meeting.acts19.us. You'll land on the sign-in page and use the username and password an administrator created for you — there's no self sign-up and no Google/Microsoft account needed.

The top bar

ButtonWhat it does
➕ New meetingUpload an audio file or record live.
👥 UsersManage accounts — only visible to administrators.
📖 DocsDownload this user guide as a PDF (the copy in the app is always current).
🔑 PasswordChange your own password.
Sign outEnds your session. Do this on shared computers.

Changing your password

  1. Click Password in the top bar.
  2. Enter your current password, then the new one twice.
  3. Click Change password — you stay signed in.
Password requirements: at least 8 characters, not all numbers, not common, and not too similar to your username — the page says so if it's rejected.

2. The meeting list

Your home page. Every meeting you own is listed with its status, length, and creation date. Click any row to open it.

StatusMeaning
📤 UploadedSaved — waiting its turn to be transcribed.
⏳ ProcessingBeing transcribed and split by speaker right now.
✅ TranscribedDone — transcript, speakers, summaries, and exports are available.
❌ FailedSomething went wrong; open the meeting to see the reason, then re-upload.
You only see your own meetings. Each account has its own list, so a shared login is never needed and nobody else's recordings appear in your account.

3. Recording or uploading a meeting

Option A — Upload an existing recording

  1. Type a title that will make sense later (for example Deacons meeting — Oct 16).
  2. Under Audio file, choose the file. MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, and video files all work — phone voice memos and Zoom recordings are fine.
  3. Click Upload & transcribe.
  4. The meeting appears in your list as ⏳ Processing. Leave the page; it keeps working. Longer recordings take longer — roughly a minute of processing per ten minutes of audio.

Option B — Record live in the browser

  1. Type a title.
  2. Click ● Start recording and allow microphone access when the browser asks (this is why the site uses a secure https:// address).
  3. Talk normally. A Live transcript preview fills in roughly every 10 seconds while you record, so you can see the transcription is working.
  4. Click ■ Stop when the meeting ends, then Upload & transcribe to run the full, higher-quality pass.
Two things happen at once while you record. The whole meeting is saved as one continuous file for the final transcript, while short preview clips are sent for the live text. The preview is a preview — the finished transcript after you stop is the one to trust.
Keep the tab open while recording — recording happens in the browser, and closing it (or locking some phones) can stop it. A "hiccup" line in the preview only means one short clip was skipped; your recording is fine.

4. Working with a meeting

Everything happens on one page. From top to bottom:

👥 Name the speakers

Diarization splits the audio into voices but can't know names, so you map them once:

  1. Click Play sample next to a speaker to hear a few seconds of that person.
  2. Type the person's name in the box (up to 100 characters).
  3. Click Save speaker names.

The transcript, the downloads, and any summary you generate afterwards all use those names. Leave a box blank to keep the original label. Naming never re-runs transcription, so it's instant.

📝 The transcript

  • Each line shows a timestamp and who spoke, colour-coded per speaker.
  • Click a timestamp to jump the audio player to that moment.
  • Use 📋 Copy to copy the whole transcript to your clipboard.
  • ⬇️ Download .txt gives a plain transcript; ⬇️ Download .md gives a formatted version for notes apps and documents.
  • 📤 Save to Google Drive writes the transcript into your Drive in one click.

5. Summary, notes, and deleting

✨ AI summary

Click ✨ Summarize with AI and the page fills in four sections:

SectionWhat you get
Key PointsThe main things discussed.
Decisions MadeWhat the group actually settled on.
Action ItemsWho does what, and when.
Open QuestionsAnything left unresolved.

Summaries are generated on the church's own AI server. Click the button again any time to regenerate — naming the speakers first makes the action items name real people.

🗒️ Your notes

Type in the Add a note… box and click Add. Notes are listed with the time you wrote them and stay attached to that meeting — handy for your own follow-ups alongside the AI summary.

🗑 Deleting a meeting

Deleting is permanent. It removes the audio file, the transcript, the summary, your notes, and the export history for that meeting — there is no recycle bin. You'll get a confirmation page listing what will be removed; only the meeting's owner can delete it.

6. Managing users (administrators only)

Click Users in the top bar. Anyone with an account can use the recorder, but only administrators see this page — regular users never see the Users button at all.

Create an account for someone

  1. In the Create account row, type the username (for example natasha) and the password you'll hand them.
  2. Click Create account.
  3. Give them the address and their password, and tell them to change it on first sign-in via the Password button.

Reset someone's password

Type a new password in the box on their row and click Set password. They keep all their existing meetings — only the password changes.

Remove an account

Click Delete on their row. This takes their meetings with it, so only delete an account when the recordings genuinely aren't needed any more.

Rules worth remembering: usernames are unique and there's no self-service "forgot password" page — an administrator resets it. New accounts can't see another person's meetings, ever.

What it doesn't do (yet)

  • Ask questions about a meeting — a chat box for "what did we decide about the budget?" is the next feature planned.
  • Rename individual transcript lines — speaker names apply to that speaker across the whole meeting, not line by line.
  • Recover a deleted meeting — deletion is permanent.
  • Scheduling or calendar links — the recorder is for meetings you record or upload yourself.

8. Good to know

How accurate is it?

Strong on clear speech and typical meeting audio; rougher with heavy cross-talk, very soft speakers, noisy rooms, or heavy jargon (Bible book names, for example). Naming the speakers afterwards clears up most of it.

Meetings with more than 8 people

Speaker separation can tell apart up to eight voices — that is the hard limit of the diarization model, and the app tells you when a recording reaches it:

Nothing breaks and no audio is lost: every word is still transcribed correctly. Only attribution changes — with more than eight voices, a few quieter people may be grouped under someone else's label, and a label can carry only one name. Name it for the person who talked most and read the transcript for the rest.

What about my privacy?

  • Audio and transcripts stay on the church's own server — nothing is sent to an outside AI provider.
  • The connection is encrypted, and recordings are only reachable by the account that owns them.
  • Pages are marked "no index", so search engines won't list them, and deleting a meeting removes its audio from the server.
